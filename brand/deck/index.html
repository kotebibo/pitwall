The October pitch video: https://youtu.be/43aFbya48PM. The ideathon pitched a manager game where races are simulated. At the hackathon the idea was rebuilt from scratch and pivoted: players drive the car themselves, and Solana verifies every lap. The October prototype was a separate JavaScript file; none of its code is in this repository. MLH rules allow arriving with an idea; all code was written during the event.

Races work the same way, plus commit-reveal: the race seed's hash is committed on-chain before setups lock and revealed after, so nobody can steer the outcome. The whole race is re-driven on the server and settled on-chain.

Determinism is what makes the proof trustworthy

120

physics ticks a second, in whole-number fixed point

0

floating-point numbers in the physics

1

physics code, shared by the browser game and the server

Because the physics uses only integers and a stored sine table, a lap driven in a browser on Windows re-drives to the exact microsecond on the Linux server. A per-attempt seed changes grip and wind slightly, so an input log can't be replayed into another attempt, and a run can't contain more ticks than the real time since it started.

Devnet payout: winner +0.036 SOL, paid by the program. SPEC: same car. OPEN: your build.

Players stake SOL from their own wallet in daily or weekly passes. With fewer than 4 entrants, everyone is refunded. The Anchor program holds the stakes in a pool account and refuses to close a pool unless players received exactly the stakes minus Pit Wall's share (capped at 20% by the program). Today it runs on devnet SOL; at launch the stakes would be USDC, after a legal review of paid-entry skill contests.

Ranked: one lap, same car, Iron to Challenger

Your first lap is your result, compared with the five drivers nearest your rating. Win rank points, climb divisions, earn medal packs.

Parts are earned, scarce and wear out, so the market can't flood

0

packs for sale: all are earned by driving

5

packs a week per player, at most

100%

of its mileage retires a part

A part's effect fades over the last 30% of its life, then it retires. Weekly part supply: 3 parts per active player, between 300 and 3,000 a week. Parts change the physics (downforce, drag, grip, weight…) and the garage shows the effect in real units, with a test drive. Scrapping a part gives R&D credits. Parts live in the database and would be minted only when traded, with the trader paying the mint.

Every sprite, sound and 3D model is made by our own code

Python draws 255 sprites, numpy synthesises 68 sounds, and Godot code builds the 3D car.

No stock assets, no AI image generators. One command rebuilds it.

About 15,700 lines of Python generators (tools/art, tools/audio, tools/brand) and 2,200 lines of GDScript for the 3D car. The engine sound is built from a V6's firing frequency; crowds are hundreds of synthesised voices; the music is sequenced in code. Helper AI agents wrote these scripts; the outputs were checked on contact sheets and iterated.
